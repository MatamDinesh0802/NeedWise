# Acme Robotics Code of Conduct

**Owner:** Alice Moreau, HR Manager
**Approved by:** Priya Raman, CEO
**Effective:** 2026-11-02
**Applies to:** All employees, contractors and interns of Acme Robotics, at our Rotterdam headquarters, at customer sites and when working remotely

## 1. Why this code exists

We build robots that work next to people in warehouses. Our customers trust the AMR-200, the AMR-500 and FleetOS with their operations and their staff's safety. That trust depends on how each of us behaves every day. This code sets out what we expect from one another. It also explains what to do when something seems wrong.

No code covers every situation. If you are unsure, ask yourself whether you would be comfortable explaining your decision to a colleague, a customer or a journalist. If the answer is no, stop and ask for advice.

## 2. Our core expectations

### 2.1 Safety first
- Never bypass, disable or weaken a robot safety feature, including speed limits, lidar zones and emergency stops, in testing or in the field without written approval from Marcus Bell (CTO).
- Report safety incidents and near-misses at customer sites to Olivia Grant (Head of Operations) within 24 hours.
- Do not ship firmware or FleetOS releases that skipped required safety validation, even under deadline pressure.

### 2.2 Respect at work
- Treat everyone with dignity: colleagues, customers, suppliers and visitors.
- Harassment, discrimination and bullying are not tolerated in any form, whether in person, in chat or on calls.
- Disagree about ideas, not people.

### 2.3 Honesty with customers
- Describe product capabilities accurately in sales material, demos and proposals. Do not overstate payload, battery life or navigation performance.
- Support commitments made through Samuel Mensah's team must reflect what we can actually deliver.

### 2.4 Protecting data and systems
- FleetOS processes customer operational data. Access it only when your role requires it.
- Follow IT security policies set by Erin Takahashi, IT Administrator. Report lost devices or suspected breaches immediately.

## 3. Conflicts of interest, gifts and hospitality

Business decisions must be made in Acme's interest, not for personal gain.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Gift from a supplier worth up to €50 | You may accept it if it is occasional and not linked to a pending decision |
| Gift from a supplier above €50 | Declare it to Finance (Hannah Schmidt's team) before or immediately after accepting |
| Cash or cash equivalents (vouchers, gift cards) | Never accept, regardless of value |
| Hospitality during an active tender or negotiation | Decline, and inform your manager |
| Financial interest in a supplier, customer or competitor | Disclose it to HR in writing |

When in doubt, declare. A declaration is never a problem. An undisclosed gift can be.

## 4. Fair competition and anti-corruption

- Never offer or accept bribes, kickbacks or improper payments, directly or through agents.
- Do not share pricing or bidding information with competitors.
- Sales activity, led by Diego Alvarez, must follow applicable competition and export-control rules for robotic equipment.

## 5. Communications and confidentiality

- Only authorised spokespeople speak to the media on Acme's behalf. Route enquiries to Grace Liu, Head of Marketing.
- Keep confidential information confidential, including roadmaps, source code, customer contracts and financial results, during and after your employment.

## 6. Reporting concerns

If you see or suspect behaviour that breaks this code, the law or our policies, please speak up. You can:

1. **Talk to your manager**, or to Alice Moreau in HR.
2. **Email ethics@acme.test.** This mailbox is monitored by HR and reviewed confidentially.
3. **Use the Speak Up form**, which lets you report anonymously. You do not need to give your name, although sharing contact details helps us follow up.

Every report is acknowledged, reviewed and investigated in proportion to its seriousness. Information is shared only with people who need it to handle the case.

### No retaliation

Acme forbids retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith. This protection still applies if the concern turns out to be unfounded. Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, exclusion, intimidation and unfair performance reviews. Anyone who retaliates will face disciplinary action. Knowingly making false reports is also a breach of this code.

## 7. Consequences

Breaches of this code may lead to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract. Where the law requires, we will also refer matters to the authorities.

Send questions about this code to HR. Wei Zhang and other engineering leads can help with questions about safety and technical practice. HR reviews this code every year, and the next review is due by 2027-11-01.

### qa-12 (dan): Do I have to declare gifts from suppliers?
- unsupported claim: A supplier gift up to €50 does not need to be declared.

> It depends on the value of the gift:
> 
> - **Up to €50:** You don't need to declare it. You may accept it if it is occasional and not linked to a pending decision [S1, S2].
> - **Above €50:** Yes, declare it to Finance (Hannah Schmidt's team), either before or immediately after accepting. In the Anti-Bribery and Gifts Policy this is done through the gift register [S1, S2].
> - **Cash, vouchers or gift cards:** Never accept them, whatever the value [S1, S2].
> - **Hospitality during an active tender or negotiation:** Decline it and inform your manager [S1, S2].
> 
> If you're in doubt, declare. A declaration is never a problem, but an undisclosed gift can be [S2].
> 
> Also, if you are involved in selecting a supplier, the contract review checklist asks you to confirm that no gifts or hospitality were accepted from that supplier during the selection [S4].

### pp-04 (bob): Can I spend a month working from my parents' place in Spain?
- unsupported claim: The month could work if part of it is taken as leave or the days are spread across the year.

> No, not as a full month. Working from another country is limited to 20 days per year and requires HR approval [S1]. A month of working days would exceed that limit, unless part of the month is taken as leave or the days are spread across the year.
> 
> If you want to work from Spain for part of the time:
> - Submit a request to HR before you travel, stating the destination, the dates and the work you plan to do [S1].
> - Approval depends on tax, social security, data protection and immigration considerations [S1].
> - Do not start working from abroad until HR has confirmed approval in writing [S1].
> - Access to customer data and FleetOS production systems from abroad must follow IT security guidance [S1].
> 
> Your regular remote work allowance of up to 3 days per week [S3] applies to remote work in general. The sources don't say it extends to working from another country, so the 20-day limit is the one that applies abroad [S1].
